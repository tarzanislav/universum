# Guide – TRACK EDITING
The **TRACK EDITING (video, audio, subtitles)** group is for editing the individual tracks of a film: cutting and rotating the picture,
fitting a dub, shifting and fixing subtitles. You **watch and listen to everything in the player** first and only then save.
The original **never changes** – the result is always a **new file** (the video in the library, subtitles next to the original ones).

!info The player is the same on all three pages: [−5 s], [+5 s], play / pause (**space bar**),
  volume, audio track choice and a jump to the previous / next subtitle.

## Video track editing
Here you **cut out the parts you don't need** (e.g. the intro or adverts), change the **aspect ratio**, **rotate** the picture,
reduce the **height** or change the **codec**.
### Cutting
1. Click [Open a video…] and choose e.g. *Vymyslený film (2026).mkv*.
2. Play the film and, where the section should **start**, click [Start of section].
3. Where the section should **end**, click [End of section] – the section is added to the list (*From*, *To*, *Length*).
4. Repeat for more sections. **Only the sections in the list are kept**; everything else is cut out.
5. At the bottom, fill in [Title:] (or use [TMDB]) and click [Save].
- **Double-click** a section to move the player to its start; [Remove] takes it off the list.
- [Whole film] clears all sections – nothing is cut out.
!tip Without [Precise cut (the picture is re-encoded)], cutting is **fast and lossless** – a section just moves
  to the nearest point where a cut is possible (usually a fraction of a second). An exact cut is frame-accurate but takes a long time.
### Picture
- **Aspect ratio** – how players display the picture; it is written without any loss of quality.
- [Rotate:] *90° clockwise*, *180°*, *90° counterclockwise*; [Height (lines):] e.g. from 2160 to 1080.
- **Re-encoding** – *H.264* or *H.265 (HEVC)* and the [Quality:] slider.
!pozor Rotating, a different height, a different codec and an exact cut **write the picture again** with the Windows encoders – this takes a long time.
  Sound and subtitles stay unchanged.

## Audio track editing
Here you **fit a dub** from another release, fix sound that lags behind, convert sound between **PAL 25 and 23.976 FPS**,
and change the codec or the volume. The chosen sound plays with the picture **exactly as it will be written**.
### Fitting a dub
1. [Open a video…] – e.g. *Vymyslený film (2026).mkv*.
2. [Add a sound…] – an audio file (MKA, AC-3, DTS, AAC, FLAC, WAV…) or **another film** to take the sound from.
3. Choose the added sound in the list and, at **Fit the selected audio to:**, pick the film's original sound.
4. Click [Fit]. A certain result (shift and FPS change) is **set automatically**.
5. Check it by playing it back and click [Save].
!info An uncertain result is not used automatically – the [Use the suggestion] button appears. Use it only after checking it in the player.
### Manual changes to the chosen sound
- **Shift** below the player: [−1000] [−100] [−10] … [+1000] ms – you hear the change straight away.
- **FPS change (PAL 25 ↔ 23.976)** – with [Preserve pitch] voices sound the same, just slower or faster.
- **Codec and volume** – [Codec:] *AC-3*, *E-AC-3*, *AAC*, *FLAC (lossless)*;
  [Stereo (downmix 5.1 to two channels)], [Normalize volume to −23 LUFS (EBU R128)], [Boost / reduce:].
- [Language:] and [Track name:]; the **Keep** column decides whether the track stays in the result.
- [Reset the changes] returns the chosen sound to its original state.
!tip A **shift on its own is written losslessly**. An FPS change, codec, stereo and volume write the sound again.

## Subtitle track editing
Here you **shift, fit, fix, download, translate** or **read subtitles from pictures**. The subtitles you are editing
are **white at the bottom** of the picture; those chosen in [Compare with:] are **yellow at the top**.
### Shifting while you watch
1. [Open a video…] (or [Open subtitles…] for subtitle files on their own).
2. On the **Subtitle tracks** tab, choose the subtitles you want to edit.
3. Play the film. When a subtitle is late, click [Subtitle starts now] at the moment the character starts speaking.
4. Fine-tune with [−100] / [+100] (ms) and click [Save].
### Automatic fitting
- Tick **by audio** and/or **by subtitles:** (other subtitles that match the film, even in another language).
- [Fit] sets the shift and also an **FPS change** or speed. An uncertain result waits for [Use the suggestion].
- [Check the video's subtitles] tells you for each track: in sync, late, early or a different FPS.
- **Synchronization by two subtitles**: at a subtitle near the start of the film click [Point 1], at a subtitle near the end
  click [Point 2] – speed and shift are worked out automatically.
### Tabs
- **Subtitle times** – all subtitles with the time *Now* and *After change*. Fix the text with the **F2** key;
  the right mouse button offers *Remove subtitle* and *Revert all text edits*.
- **Download and translate** – OpenSubtitles.com, titulky.com via the browser and translation via svsub.net.
  The result is saved next to the video, e.g. *Vymyslený film (2026).sk.srt*.
- **Text from images (OCR)** – turns picture subtitles from Blu-ray (PGS) and DVD (VobSub) into text: choose the track and click
  [Recognize the text]. When it meets a shape it doesn't know, it **asks once per film** and remembers it.
![Subtitle track editing](studio-subtitles.jpg)
![OCR asks about an unknown shape](ocr-question.jpg)
!info When OCR asks, type the letter and press [Next]; a smudge is [Not a letter], and if you are unsure, [Don't know].
  [Correct the text by language] then fixes doubtful words – you can [Revert] any fix.
!pozor Subtitle files are saved as **new SRT or WebVTT files next to the original ones**, never over an existing file.

## Using the studio from Batch processing
You can edit a file from Batch processing in the studio **before the job starts**:
1. In Batch processing, choose the file and click [Subtitles in studio] or [Audio in studio]
   (or right-click: *Edit in studio (subtitles)* / *Edit in studio (audio)*).
2. The file opens in Subtitle track editing or Audio track editing, with the changes made so far.
3. Change what you need and click [Save to the job]. The changes are written **only when the job runs**.
- [Back without saving] goes back without any changes.
- Outside a job, you can put unfinished work aside with [Save the work] – the next time you open **the same video**
  it comes back. Nothing is written to the files; [Discard the work] forgets it.
!pozor **Subtitle text fixes** (F2, elements for the deaf, formatting, overlaps) cannot be saved to a job –
  the studio warns you about this. Save such subtitles directly with [Save].

## Tips
- **Listen first, save later** – check every change in the player; it plays straight away exactly as it will be written.
- [Clear] forgets the open video – nothing changes on the disk.
- Before [Save] you can switch on [Apply the UNIVERSUM rules (track order, flags, Forced, removing empty ones)].
!tip A dub from a PAL release (25 FPS) for a film at 23.976 FPS: [Fit] finds the FPS change too – no need to enter it by hand.
!tip Subtitles from OpenSubtitles show whether the **FPS matches** the film – pick the ones that match.
